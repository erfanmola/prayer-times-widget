The five daily prayers

The widget lists Fajr (dawn), Dhuhr (after midday), Asr (afternoon), Maghrib (just after sunset) and Isha (night), plus sunrise, which marks the end of the Fajr time. Each time is worked out from the sun's position at your location on today's date, which is why prayer times change every day and from city to city.

Calculation methods

Organisations differ mainly in the sun angles they use for Fajr and Isha. You can pick the method your mosque or community follows:

  • Muslim World League
  • Egyptian General Authority
  • University of Islamic Sciences, Karachi
  • Umm al-Qura, Makkah
  • Islamic Society of North America
  • Institute of Geophysics, Tehran
  • Diyanet, Turkey
  • Moonsighting Committee, and the Dubai, Kuwait, Qatar and Singapore methods

For Asr, choose the standard method (Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali) or the Hanafi method, which starts Asr later. Details are on the methodology page.

Frequently asked questions

Which calculation method should I choose?

Follow your local mosque if it publishes a timetable. Otherwise the Muslim World League method is a widely used default, and regional authorities such as Umm al-Qura, ISNA or Diyanet are common in their regions.

Why are my times a few minutes different from my mosque's?

Mosques may use a different calculation method, a different Asr convention, or add a few minutes of safety margin. Pick the same method as your mosque to match it closely.

What is the difference between standard and Hanafi Asr?

Asr begins when an object's shadow reaches a set multiple of its height. The standard method uses one times the height, and the Hanafi method uses twice the height, so Hanafi Asr starts later.

Do I need to share my location?

The widget uses your location to calculate times for exactly where you are. To look up another city, use the full prayer times page, which has a city search. Location is only used to calculate results and to look up the name of your city; no account is needed. See the QiblaFind privacy policy.
